Most reels flop on one trigger. These are the 35 we build every hook with.

By Savage Social  ·  2,700 words  ·  13 min read

Open your own feed and count the triggers in the first two seconds of each video. Most have one. Plenty have zero, which is exactly why they got 400 views and a polite like from your aunt.

A trigger is anything that fires the viewer's nervous system, positively or negatively. Comparison, fear, intrigue, common enemy, belonging, super confidence, social proof, vividness, exclusivity, simplicity, FOMO, pattern, surprise, dissonance, result, novelty, challenge, specificity, future glimpse, scarcity. Content without triggers is bland content. Most copy that flops flops because it has zero or only one trigger doing the work.

This is the full taxonomy we write with: 35 triggers across three categories, how many each format needs, the stacks that convert, and the quality check we run before anything goes out.

How many triggers each format needs

These are minimums, not stretch goals.

  • Reel script (30 to 60 seconds): 4 triggers minimum. Ideally one every 5 to 7 seconds.
  • Caption: 2 minimum, 3 or 4 ideal.
  • Email subject plus opener: 2 minimum (one in the subject, one in the opener), 3 ideal.
  • Threads or X post: 1 strong trigger, 2 layered.
  • Ad headline: 1 trigger, 2 layered.
  • LinkedIn post hook: 1 in line 1, 1 in line 2, 3 in the first 3 lines.

Stack triggers, never bet on one. The common enemy trigger alone gets weak engagement now. Same with fear alone, FOMO alone. Combine.

The three categories

Individual triggers are tied to who the creator is, so they are not universal. Technical triggers are visual or audio, and they live in the production rather than the script. Standard triggers are the universal patterns that work everywhere: site copy, stories, reels, emails, ads.

Two ways to write with them. Texture first: draft the raw idea you want to convey, then layer triggers in. Trigger first: pick a trigger or format, then find the texture that fits. Both work. Sometimes a draft that is already clean just needs a trigger pass on the headline and CTA.

The 35 triggers

Category

Individual triggers

What this specific creator broadcasts that makes their audience react. Common axes: money, success, looks, health, vibrancy of life, energy, relationships, friendships, freedom, super confidence, results, individual hobbies, family setup, travel patterns, recognition, big-name clients, team, ease of creating content, ideational bent. These belong in the main idea slot of a reel, the thesis line. The exercise for any creator: list the things you broadcast that make your audience react, and rotate them through main idea slots.

Category

Technical triggers

A strong hook (a visually arresting first frame). A scroll-stopping sound. Visual intrigue, something half revealed or unusual on screen. Something hypnotic or "eat the screen": loops, satisfying motion, ASMR moments. Contrast, especially before and after with a strong visual delta. A script with weak verbal triggers can still work if the production carries strong technical ones. The inverse, weak production saved by strong words, only works if the words are spectacular.

Standard

Comparison

Setting two things against each other so one looks better, worse, or just defined by the contrast. Useful for ICP filtering, value props, before and after results, generational humor. Patterns: "How 20-somethings X vs how 40-somethings X." "I tried [popular option] for a year and switched to [your option] in a week." "Then vs now."

Standard

Analogy

The most creative trigger. Compare your subject to something unexpected. "This resume is a Tinder profile, not a resume." "Your ad creative is a screensaver, not a hook."

Standard

Belonging and situations

Put the reader inside a category they identify with. "Every SMM specialist should..." "If you're an entrepreneur, you want..." "Moms of three know exactly what I mean when..."

Standard

FOMO

Fear of missing out, missing a window, missing a moment. "Summer's over and you still haven't tried..." "Final week before [thing] gets archived." Intensifying words: even, will catch up to you, still, don't wait, soon, while it's still relevant, right now.

Standard

Simplicity and ease

How fast or easy something is to do or get. "One minute and..." "One click..." "The 30-second version..." Intensifying words: simply, easily, just a few steps, effortlessly, instantly, no problem, fast, even a beginner can, do it easily and quickly.

Standard

Immediate benefit

The result is right now, not weeks away. Often paired with understatement of the effort: "It only took me [tiny effort]." Even reels with modest views (10K) often use this.

Standard

Personal address and presence

Speaking directly to one reader. "You" not "people." Eye contact on camera. Names if you know them. The feeling of presence is a trigger by itself.

Standard

Super confidence

Speaking from total certainty. Praising yourself without softening. "I'm the best at this." "I know exactly what you need." "You're lucky." "Take this gift from fate." "I'm sure." "I know." "There's nothing to debate." "It's obvious." Use hyperboles and bold comparisons.

Standard

Proof, guarantees, and social proof

Specific, named, citable proof. Intensifying words: our clients confirm, real results, not just words but facts, look at what others are saying, tested and confirmed, what we set out to prove. Social proof is the sibling version: evidence that other people have already done this, the "I have clients" type of proof. It usually lives in the body or the description, not the headline.

Standard

Common enemy and stereotype

Position yourself and the reader against something: a stereotype, a habit, a system, a behavior. Make the enemy a problem or a situation, not a category of people, or the hate boomerangs onto you. Specificity is critical. "Stress" is too vague. "The stress before a first date" lands.

Standard

Authority

Three layers. Credentials: "I ran 1000 consultations, here's what I can tell you." "I studied everything in marketing, and what I can say is..." Expert citations inside the reel: quoting an expert, borrowing a clip from a known creator and commenting on it, linking research, certificates, awards, experience, achievements, press features, brand collaborations, education. Quantity: "I tested 200 of these." Quantity is instant authority.

Standard

Result

A specific outcome you achieved or your audience wants. "How I gained 100 followers in..." "How I made $X..." Must combine with other intensifying triggers, because result alone is weak.

Standard

Fear

Fear of not getting something. Fear of consequences. "You'll never achieve this." "Something might happen to you." Intensifying words: beware, be careful, watch out, risk, threat, with caution, don't ignore, avoid. Visuals and sound amplify fear.

Standard

Repeat

Asking the reader to do something simple alongside you. "Repeat after me if this is you." "Can you do this too?" Intensifying words: imagine, try, repeat.

Standard

Pattern and sequence

Structure makes things easier to absorb. Numbering, ordering, "first A then B." Patterns: "Let's count." "First A then B." "There are exactly 3 stages, and most people skip stage 2."

Standard

Vividness, show don't tell

Demonstrate visually what you're saying. Showing tights and intentionally tearing them to prove they don't tear. Holding the product up while saying its name. Splitting the screen with two outcomes. Intensifying words: look, see, watch, take a look at, examine, right in front of you, compare, imagine. Teaser visuals, showing without revealing, also live here.

Standard

Dissonance

Combine two things that shouldn't go together. Create an illusion. Make the reader double-take. A serious doctor in a beach setting talking about emergency procedures. A florist arranging a bouquet while telling a cheating story. A finance video set in a kitchen.

Standard

Star and celebrity

Tying yourself or your subject to a recognized name. Borrowing recognition. "Natural brows like Natalia Vodianova." "The technique Jennifer Lopez uses for her wishlist."

Standard

Habit and consistency

"As I told you," "as I promised," "I've been doing this for years," "I'll keep doing it the same way," "not the first time," "by tradition." Builds the feeling that you're the source of an ongoing series.

Standard

Herd, everyone's doing it

The pull of being like everyone, the pain of being left out. Intensifying words: everyone, everybody, many, most, popular, it's everywhere, trend, always.

Standard

Opposition and uniqueness

The opposite of herd. Standing apart. Intensifying words: don't be like everyone, unique, unlike others, no one else does this, compared to the rest, unique approach.

Standard

Exclusivity and chosen-ness

"This isn't for everyone." "Some people won't be able to do this." Words: exclusive, closed, original, rare. Privilege.

Standard

Audience interaction

Replying to the audience IS content. Replying to a comment in the headline. Screenshots of conversations. Recreating a DM as a sketch.

Standard

Novelty and secrets

The trigger of "new." First time, new, fresh, you haven't heard this yet.

Standard

Challenge

"Let's do this." "Let's try." "I dare you to..."

Standard

Specificity, numbers, detail

Concrete numbers, weights, dates, durations, dollar amounts. A vague number is weak. "$10,000" beats "a lot." "21 minutes" beats "a few minutes." "5 years and 3 months" beats "a long time." Categories that hit hard: time, money, weight, experience.

Standard

Future glimpse

Place the reader in the future where they've already gotten what you're offering. From a travel brand: "Imagine you're lying in Cannes right now, drinking a $10 mojito, and the tickets here cost you $200 because you booked them on [our site]."

Standard

Intrigue

A hint without resolution. "There's one specific thing I do that nobody else does..." Don't kill the intrigue in the same beat. Make them stay for it.

Standard

Surprise and the unexpected

The unexpected combination, the unexpected reversal, the unexpected ending. Often pairs with dissonance.

Standard

Contradiction and push-away

"Don't read this." "Don't watch this video." "Skip this if you're not ready." The forbidden makes them stay.

Standard

Be part of something bigger

"We're not just selling X..." Position the offering as a movement, a cause, a community. Extra benefits beyond the product itself.

Standard

Scarcity

Limited supply. "12 spots in the cohort." "Only 3 of these in stock."

Standard

Reciprocity

Give something first: a free guide, a useful insight, a free workshop. The reader feels the obligation to give back attention or money.

How triggers stack

The best copy stacks triggers. These are the combinations we reach for most:

  • Comparison plus result: "How 20-somethings save vs 40-somethings save (one of these gets to retirement)."
  • Common enemy plus specificity: "The stress before a job interview is a different animal than work stress, and most people fix the wrong one."
  • Authority plus quantity plus simplicity: "After 100 client launches I can tell you which 2 things move the needle in week one."
  • FOMO plus scarcity plus result: "12 spots, closes Friday, last cohort booked out in 4 days, average client added $30K in 90 days."
  • Dissonance plus future glimpse plus super confidence: "I'm a tax accountant and I'm telling you to spend more this quarter, here's the math, and yes, I'm right."
  • Belonging plus common enemy plus super confidence: "If you're a marketer who hates the 'post every day' advice, you're going to like the next 30 seconds."

Where each trigger goes in a reel

Every section of a reel script needs its own trigger, and they should not be the same one repeated.

  • Headline (on screen and voiceover, first 1 to 2 seconds): the strongest trigger you have. It appears immediately as one block, not broken across two takes, not delayed by a "hi, my name is" intro.
  • Main idea (the thesis): an individual trigger, something distinctive to you or the client's identity.
  • Retention (the body): triggers every 5 to 7 seconds. Comparison, vividness, dissonance.
  • CTA: a trigger that motivates action. FOMO, simplicity, exclusivity, future glimpse.
  • Description (the caption): a trigger that pulls the people who skipped the audio. It is a second headline, not a copy of the first.

On CTAs specifically: a CTA that just says "follow" or "comment" converts near zero. Leading the CTA with a trigger lifts follow conversion by 50 to 60 percent. Lead with the result or the pain the reader will solve, not with a button name. "Comment X if you want the [specific result]." "Save this before it gets archived." "If you're sick of [thing], this guide is yours, comment X."

What weak trigger use looks like

  • Only one trigger across the whole piece
  • A trigger in the script but missing from the visual, or the reverse
  • Vague enemies, vague pains, vague promises
  • Triggers in the wrong slots, like a common enemy trigger in the CTA where you need FOMO or simplicity
  • A trigger that contradicts the visual, claiming "ease" while the demo looks complicated
  • Two triggers fighting each other, like FOMO plus super relaxed pacing

What strong trigger use looks like

  • A trigger in the first 2 seconds, no warmup
  • A trigger every 5 to 7 seconds through retention
  • Each trigger in a different voice, a different angle every time
  • Specificity throughout: names, numbers, situations
  • A CTA that leads with a trigger, not a verb
  • A description that is its own trigger, not a repeat of the headline

The formula for takeoff

Triggers plus texture plus relevance plus consistency.

A great trigger on weak texture flops. Great texture with no triggers flops. Both with no relevance to what is hot right now flops. All three with no consistency means one viral hit and a dead account. All four is takeoff.

Texture is the underlying fact, story, or angle, and it has to qualify before you layer triggers on. Good texture leads to a specific business goal, is not egocentric, is not obvious or banal, is alive, is specific with concrete numbers and names and situations, gives a solution or a state, reflects real understanding of who you're talking to, has individuality, and catches a pattern the reader has noticed but could never articulate.

The check we run before anything ships

  1. Trigger count. Does the piece hit the minimum for its format? Four in a reel, three in a caption.
  2. First beat. Is there a trigger in the very first 1 to 2 seconds or the first sentence? If not, it fails.
  3. Specificity. Is the enemy, pain, or promise concrete, with numbers or named situations?
  4. Texture. Does the underlying fact pass the texture test?
  5. Stacked triggers. Are at least two different triggers working at once, not one repeated?
  6. CTA trigger. Does the CTA lead with a trigger instead of a generic "follow" or "save"?
  7. Banner blindness. Does any line match a dead headline template? "How to," "Top 5," "Tips for," "Benefits of," "Last chance." If yes, rewrite.

If any check fails, rewrite the failing part before it goes out. Not after it underperforms.

Frequently asked questions

What is a trigger in social media content?

Anything that fires the viewer's nervous system, positively or negatively. Comparison, fear, intrigue, common enemy, belonging, super confidence, social proof, vividness, exclusivity, simplicity, FOMO, pattern, surprise, dissonance, result, novelty, challenge, specificity, future glimpse, scarcity. Content without triggers is bland content, and most copy that flops flops because it has zero or only one trigger doing the work.

How many triggers should a reel script have?

Four minimum for a 30 to 60 second reel, ideally one every five to seven seconds. A caption needs at least two, ideally three or four. An email needs one in the subject line and one in the opener. An ad headline needs at least one, ideally two layered.

Why does the common enemy trigger sometimes backfire?

Because the enemy was a category of people instead of a problem or a situation. When the creator visibly judges a group, the hate boomerangs back onto the creator. The other failure is vagueness. "Stress" is too vague to land. "The stress before a job interview" or "the stress before a first date" lands.

Can one trigger carry a whole post?

Not anymore. Common enemy alone gets weak engagement now, and so does fear alone or FOMO alone. The feed has seen every single trigger used by itself a million times. Stacking two or three in different voices is what still works.
